The project

A textbook that does the mathematics while you read it

Fable is a complete school publishing system: an engine that builds interactive textbooks, a studio to write them in, a pupil's notebook, and the experiments lifted out to stand on their own. It is built, it runs, and everything on this site was made with it.

A figure in a textbook is a photograph of a thought

Someone drew it once, at one set of values, and printed it. It cannot be asked anything. A pupil who wonders what happens at a different angle, a different concentration, a different coefficient has nowhere to put the question — and that question is the moment the lesson was working.

The digital editions mostly reproduce the problem at greater expense: a PDF of the same picture, or a video of one run chosen by someone else. Both are recordings. Neither can be interrogated, and a recording cannot be wrong in an interesting way.

Fable's figures are solved, on the page, from the same few lines of mathematics the lesson is about. That is the whole idea, and everything else here follows from it.

The parts

The engine

builds a book

Takes chapters written in a readable text format and produces one self-contained HTML file per book — figures, styles and scripts inside it. Nothing to install to read one, and nothing to keep running to serve it.

Studio

where a book is written

An authoring environment for teachers: chapters, the 102 figure types with a form for each, themes, and a build button. It writes files and runs builds, so it stays on the author's own machine.

Le Jaguar

the pupil's notebook

Séyès paper in a browser. Clippings arrive from any book with their source attached. Everything stays on the device; an account is optional and only adds a copy that travels.

The console

what goes online

One button builds and publishes the public site, with a rights guard it deliberately cannot override: a scanned book cannot be published by accident, because a book published by accident cannot be unpublished.

A cursor works each one for you — pressing a tab, choosing from a list, dragging a slider — and every one of those gestures is a real event on a real control, which is why the figure answers. Touch it and the cursor stands down: from then on you are the one driving.

What it runs on

A browser, and nothing else.

Every book is a single HTML file. It opens on the school's tablets, on a ten-year-old laptop, from a memory stick, and it keeps working when the connection drops mid-lesson — which, in most of the schools this is meant for, it will. There is no app to approve, no account to create for a thirteen-year-old, and no server whose invoice can end the lesson.

Who it is for

Schools

A department adopts one subject's book for one year group. The teacher can edit it — that is the point of Studio — so the book follows the way they already teach instead of the other way round.

  • Runs on what the school already owns.
  • Pupils' writing stays on pupils' devices.
  • Le Classeur covers the register, reports and exam plans.

Training programmes

Anywhere a concept is easier moved than described: apprenticeships, technical certificates, in-house training. The figure types are not school-specific — a titration curve and a load calculation are the same kind of object.

  • Course material that can be handed over as one file.
  • Written by the trainer, not commissioned from an agency.

Publishers

An existing catalogue becomes interactive without being rewritten: the import pipeline takes a PDF or an EPUB and produces chapters, and the figures are then placed by hand where they earn their place.

  • The rights guard refuses to publish what is not ours to publish.
  • Output is a file you own, not a licence you rent.

What is next

Everything above can be checked against the build. Everything in this section cannot — it is yours to fill in, and it has been left visibly blank rather than filled with a plausible number. A pitch page that invents its own traction is the one mistake that cannot be repaired later.

In use today

Nowhere yet. It is built, it runs, and every page of this site came out of it — but no class has used it in a lesson. The first school to try it will be the first.

What support would buy

A team. This is one person’s work, and one person cannot cover the official Moroccan programmes subject by subject and year by year. Support pays the people who would, and the bills while they do it.

Who is behind it

Mohamed Amine Khadda, on his own so far. The argument is short: a child’s attention is competed for by everything now, and a textbook that cannot answer a question is not in that competition. If school is going to hold them, it has to be as good at holding them as what it is up against.
